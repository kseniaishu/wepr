Method and limits

What this map is, and is not

Where it is solid
{{ summary.cells_interpolated }} cells lie inside the convex hull of the cities, so they sit between observations. The {{ summary.cells_extrapolated }} translucent cells do not: every city is on one side of them, which makes them extrapolation, not interpolation.
How far the evidence reaches
The furthest drawn cell is {{ summary.max_distance_to_city_km }} km from any city. Twenty cities cannot resolve a 9 km field, and the markers are left on the map so the sparsity is visible rather than described.
{% if summary.surface %}
How it interpolates
Not by distance alone. A surface in elevation, latitude and longitude is fitted first — it explains R² = {{ summary.surface.r2 }} of the spread between cities, at {{ summary.surface.lapse_rate_c_per_km }} {{ summary.units }} per kilometre of elevation — and only the residuals, with a standard deviation of {{ summary.surface.residual_sd }} {{ summary.units }}, are distance-weighted. Weighting raw values would paint the Ebro valley's temperature onto the Pyrenean ridges above it.
Where the physics is extrapolating
The surface was fitted on cities between {{ summary.surface.fitted_elevation_range_m[0] }} m and {{ summary.surface.fitted_elevation_range_m[1] }} m. The hatched cells sit above that, so the lapse rate is being applied beyond the ground it was measured on.
{% endif %}
Where nothing is drawn
Over the sea, more than {{ summary.hull_margin_km }} km beyond the city hull, and off the cities' own landmass. North Africa is excluded by that last rule rather than by distance: the Strait of Gibraltar is 14 km wide, so no distance threshold separates Tarifa from Tetouan.
