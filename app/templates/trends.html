This page shows two things side by side and refuses to merge them. The first is the warming rate measured in this record, with its uncertainty, extrapolated forward and labelled as the statistical extrapolation it is. The second is the published CMIP6 scenario envelope, quoted with its source. Extrapolating a line fitted to a reanalysis record is not climate projection; real projection uses model ensembles under emissions scenarios. Knowing the difference is the point.

Measured here · statistical

The observed rate, city by city

Each dot is a Theil–Sen slope in °C per decade; each bar is its 95% confidence interval. Theil–Sen rather than ordinary least squares because a long daily record contains outlier years, and a robust estimator does not let a handful of them set the slope — the OLS slope is in the table below so the choice is auditable rather than asserted.

The line at zero is the thing to watch. An interval that touches it is an interval containing no warming at all, which means the sign of the slope is not established. {% set sound = summary.cities | selectattr('is_sound') | list %} {% if sound | length == 0 %} On this record every one of the {{ summary.n_cities }} intervals crosses zero. Carried forward, which is arithmetic rather than physics

The same fitted line evaluated at two future years. The first interval is the slope's own confidence interval swung about the end of the record, so it has no width where the data ends and widens with distance. The second adds 90% of the record's interannual scatter, which is the band a single future year would fall in — it does not shrink to nothing, because individual years scatter about a trend even when the trend is known exactly.

Baselines

Why these two are not merged

The first reason is what they are. One is measured in this record and one is quoted from a model ensemble; drawing them as a single series would hide that, and the difference is the entire point of the page.

The second is arithmetic, and it settles the question outright for some of the rows. Every scenario figure above is an anomaly against its own baseline period, so putting one on the same axis as a local extrapolation means expressing both against that same baseline — which requires the local record to actually cover it.
